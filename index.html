At the Airport

Customs & Entry — Bring a $10 Bill!

💵 Bring a crisp US $10 bill per person U.S. citizens are charged $10 each for a tourist card upon entry — and the customs officers usually don't have change. Have exact change ready in your passport to breeze through. (Make sure the bill is in perfect condition — no rips, tears, or stains!)

You'll also fill out a short entry form. Have the name and address of your hotel in Granada handy — you'll be asked where you're staying. Make sure your passport is valid for at least six months beyond your travel dates.

Important

What NOT to Bring

Nicaragua strictly restricts certain items at the border. These will be confiscated (and may cause you a long delay at customs), so please leave them at home:

  • 🚫Drones — absolutely prohibited, no exceptions.
  • 🚫Walkie-talkies / two-way radios — any radio communication equipment is prohibited.
  • 🚫Knives or weapons of any kind — including decorative, inert, or multi-tool blades.
  • 🚫Recreational drugs — of any kind, full stop. Penalties are severe.
  • ⚠️Professional camera gear & binoculars — large professional photography/video equipment and binoculars can be seized at entry. Regular phones and ordinary personal cameras are fine.
  • ⚠️Camouflage or military-style clothing — best avoided.
And one golden rule while you're here: Do not say, post, wear, or bring anything political while in Nicaragua. Keep conversations to travel, food, the wedding, and fun — and you'll have a wonderful, trouble-free trip.
Peace of Mind

Nervous About Visiting? A Note from Nick

If this is your first time in Nicaragua or Central America and the travel alerts have you worried — take a breath. I've been traveling to Nicaragua for over 8 years, and in my experience the alerts are overstated. Granada is a welcoming, well-touristed city where visitors are treated warmly.

Use the same common sense you would anywhere: stick to well-traveled areas, keep an eye on your belongings, follow the "nothing political, no drugs" rules above, and you'll be absolutely fine.

Worried about the language? Don't be. English is very widely spoken in Granada, especially in hotels, restaurants, and anywhere in the hospitality industry. A friendly "hola" and "gracias" go a long way, but you won't be stuck.

Health

Mosquitoes & Staying Healthy

There is currently a mosquito-borne illness (chikungunya) circulating in Nicaragua, and dengue is also present in the region. The good news: Granada does a very good job with mosquito prevention these days, and simple precautions keep your risk low.

  • Wear bug spray — use a repellent with 20%+ DEET (or picaridin) and reapply through the day.
  • Cover up at dusk and dawn — long sleeves and long pants in the evenings and early mornings, when mosquitoes are most active.
  • Sleep smart — air-conditioned or screened rooms (standard at Granada hotels) keep mosquitoes out.
  • Vaccines — for a short trip like this one, the travel vaccine generally isn't considered necessary, but check with your doctor if you have any health concerns.
💊 If you get a fever while traveling: Take acetaminophen (Tylenol) only. Avoid ibuprofen (Advil) and aspirin until dengue is ruled out, as they can increase bleeding risk with dengue. See a doctor if a fever doesn't pass.

Also: drink bottled or filtered water, and ease into the street food. Pharmacies in Granada are plentiful and well stocked if you need anything.

Good to Know

Practical Tips

  • Supplies: Basic supplies — toiletries, sunscreen, bug spray, snacks, medicine — are easily available in Granada. Don't stress about forgetting something.
  • Money: Credit cards and tap-to-pay are widely accepted in Granada's restaurants, hotels, and shops. The local currency is the córdoba, and ATMs are available in Granada to take out local cash — we recommend using the BAC or Lafise bank ATMs (both marked 🏧 on the map above, a couple of blocks from the central park). US dollars are also widely accepted, but only bills in perfect condition — zero rips, tears, or stains — or they will be rejected. Keep some cash for street vendors and small purchases, and try to get small bills — taxis and vendors often can't make change. Having a hard time getting córdobas? Ask Nick — he'll have lots of cash on hand and can get you córdobas; just pay him back on Venmo.
  • Taxis: Taxis are easy and cheap to take around Granada — about 30–40 córdobas per person for rides around town, though they may try to charge tourists more. Nick's tip: have 40 córdobas in your hand, tell the driver where you're going, and show them the 40 — settled up front. They often don't have change, so carry small bills.
  • Weather: Hot and tropical — pack light, breathable clothing, a hat, sunscreen, and comfortable walking shoes for the cobblestone streets. Plus those long sleeves for evenings!
  • Power: Same outlets and voltage as the US — no adapters needed.
  • Phones: Check with your carrier about international plans, or grab a cheap local SIM/eSIM. Most hotels and cafés have Wi-Fi.
